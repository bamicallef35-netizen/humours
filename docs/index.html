humours

a plan grown from its inhabitants' humours / premise after R&Sie(n), Une architecture des humeurs, Le Laboratoire, Paris, 2010 / source / benmicallef

Six inhabitants are interviewed, as numbers: a stress, an empathy, and a sympathy for each of the others. Each inhabitant then claims a room, one cell at a time, from a seed on the corridor's flank. Every candidate cell is scored under that inhabitant's humours and one is drawn by softmax. Stress scores enclosure and distance from the corridor; calm scores exposure and the corridor's edge; sympathy pulls growth toward the rooms it likes and antipathy pushes it from the rooms it does not; empathy raises the appetite for any contact at all. The plan finishes when the area is spent.

A stand-in for structure: no room cell may lie further than a fixed depth from the corridor, every room stays 4-connected by construction, and every room keeps at least one cell on the corridor, so access holds after every step. Doors are read off the finished plan (one per room to the corridor, one between any two touching rooms whose sympathy is above the openness threshold) and are drawn on the sheet, which is the output.

Une architecture des humeurs, 2010

Une architecture des humeurs (Le Laboratoire, Paris, from 22 January 2010) read visitors' body chemistry and interpreted it as humours; the readings set deliberately uncertain protocols for a collective residential structure; François Jouve's recursive structural optimisation generated the morphologies; a secretion machine, Viab02, was to extrude the components in bio-cement. None of that is reproduced here. The optimisation, the chemistry and the machine are absent; the humours are seeded numbers and read no one; the structural rule is a depth limit on a grid. What is kept is the premise, that a plan can be the consequence of its inhabitants' moods run through a procedure, and the scenario format of the drawing.
